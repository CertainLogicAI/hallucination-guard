# CertainLogic Strategic Principles

> Fast-reference for decisions. If a choice violates these, it needs explicit justification.
>
> Full context: [[concepts/certainlogic-moat-thesis]]

## The Hierarchy

1. **Data flywheel over feature parity.** Every new capability should feed the corpus that makes us harder to replicate.
2. **Integration depth over integration breadth.** One workflow we own beats ten we touch.
3. **Trust compounds; hype decays.** Public artifacts (free tools, honest benchmarks, clear docs) build the moat. Growth hacks destroy it.
4. **Speed of iteration is the only sustainable tech moat.** Do it once, do it right — but ship fast. Perfectionism is a form of slowness.

## Decision Filters

### Should we build this feature?
- Does it deepen the data flywheel? → Yes, prioritize.
- Does it lock in a workflow/system-of-record position? → Yes, prioritize.
- Is it purely feature-parity or “nice to have”? → No, defer.
- Can a copycat replicate it in <4 weeks without our corpus? → No, reject.

### Should we partner?
- Does the partner deepen integration depth for our users? → Yes.
- Is this primarily for distribution we don’t control? → No. Build our own.
- Does it create data we can’t own? → No. Data moats must stay central.

### Should we open-source?
- Does open-sourcing accelerate the data flywheel (more contributors → better corpus)? → Consider carefully.
- Does it replicate our moat for competitors without cost? → No.
- Is the code infrastructure-layer (not app-layer)? → Different calculus — see [[concepts/open-source-strategy]] (if exists).

### Should we patent?
- See month-6 IP attorney consult decision. Until then: trade secret.
- The Deterministic AI Brain is **NOT filed**. Keep it that way until legal review.

## Red Lines

- No “AI-powered” wrapper products without outcome-aligned data underneath.
- No partnerships that fragment our data ownership.
- No feature bloat justified by “competitive parity.” If they have it and we don’t, that’s often a signal we’re on the right track.

## Application

These principles are **active context** for:
- Product roadmap prioritization
- Partnership evaluation
- Hiring and team structure
- Pricing and packaging decisions
- Content and public positioning

When in doubt, default to the moat thesis. Short-term revenue < long-term defensibility.
