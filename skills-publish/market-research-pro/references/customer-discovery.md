# Customer Discovery Interview Guide

## Before the Interview

### Who to Talk To
- **Current customers** — why they bought, what they'd change
- **Churned customers** — why they left (most valuable feedback)
- **Prospects who said no** — what blocked the deal
- **People in your ICP who don't know you** — unbiased pain discovery
- **Power users of competitors** — what they love and hate

### How Many
- **5 interviews** reveals ~80% of usability issues (Nielsen)
- **12-15 interviews** for robust pattern detection
- **20+ interviews** for quantitative confidence
- Stop when you hear the same answers 3 times in a row (saturation)

### Rules
1. **Never pitch.** You're here to learn, not sell.
2. **Ask about the past, not the future.** "Tell me about the last time you..." not "Would you use..."
3. **Shut up.** Aim for 80% them talking, 20% you.
4. **Record everything** (with permission). Notes miss nuance.
5. **Follow the energy.** When they get emotional or detailed, dig deeper.

## The Interview Script

### Opening (2 min)
```
Thanks for your time. I'm researching [topic area] and trying to understand 
how people like you handle [general problem]. No right or wrong answers — 
I'm just trying to learn.

Mind if I record this for my notes? Only for internal use.
```

### Context (3 min)
```
1. What's your role, and what does a typical day/week look like?
2. How does [problem area] fit into your work?
3. How long have you been dealing with [problem]?
```

### Pain Discovery (10 min)
```
4. Walk me through the last time you had to [task related to problem]. 
   What happened?
5. What was the hardest part?
6. How did you solve it? (or try to?)
7. How much time/money does this cost you [per week/month/quarter]?
8. What have you tried before? What worked, what didn't?
9. On a scale of 1-10, how painful is this? Why not a [lower number]? 
   Why not a [higher number]?
```

### Solution Exploration (5 min)
```
10. If you could wave a magic wand and fix one thing about [problem], 
    what would it be?
11. What would a perfect solution look like for you?
12. What tools/services have you looked at? What did you think?
13. What would make you switch from your current approach?
```

### Willingness to Pay (3 min)
```
14. How much are you currently spending on this? (tools, time, people)
15. If something cut [pain] in half, what would that be worth to you?
16. What would make you say "shut up and take my money"?
```

### Closing (2 min)
```
17. Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?
18. Who else should I talk to about this? (referral = gold)
19. Mind if I follow up if I build something based on what I learned?
```

### Pattern Detection
After 5+ interviews, look for:
- **Repeated words/phrases** — this is their language (use it in copy)
- **Common workarounds** — this is your competition
- **Emotional spikes** — this is where the money is
- **Willingness to pay floor** — lowest number anyone said
- **Deal breakers** — features/aspects that are non-negotiable

## Anti-Patterns (The Mom Test)

From Rob Fitzpatrick's "The Mom Test" — questions that produce lies:

| ❌ Bad Question | Why It Fails | ✅ Better Question |
|----------------|-------------|-------------------|
| "Would you use this?" | Everyone says yes to be nice | "How do you handle this today?" |
| "Do you think it's a good idea?" | Opinions ≠ behavior | "When's the last time this was a problem?" |
| "How much would you pay?" | Hypothetical = inflated | "How much are you spending now?" |
| "Would you buy this?" | Future promises mean nothing | "Can I take your payment info now?" |

**The rule:** Talk about their life, not your idea. Past behavior predicts future behavior. Compliments are noise.
